ATHENA: HOW THE RESEARCH REVIEW PROCESS WORKS
Based on the local application's current implementation, 18 October 2026.

This guide explains who acts, what they do, and who receives the work next.
It describes the app as it works today. The clarity suggestions near the end
are suggestions, not changes already made to the app.


1. THE OVERALL FLOW

Proposal:
Faculty -> Research Head -> GAD assessment -> Co-evaluator review ->
LREC presentation/review -> Final signing -> Signed Notice to Proceed

Quarterly project reports:
Faculty Researcher -> Research Office Secretary -> Research Head

Campus reporting:
Eligible faculty evidence -> Coordinator's College Accomplishment Report ->
Research Head's consolidated RMS Excel report

These are connected processes, but they have different review rules.
A proposal clearance, a report acceptance, and project completion are three
different decisions.


2. WHO DOES WHAT

Faculty / project owner:
Creates the proposal, works with collaborators, and submits the package.
After document release, uses the Faculty Researcher workspace to report on
the approved project. The owner submits the prepared project reports.

Research Head:
Reviews proposals, records assessment results, requests revisions, rejects
proposals, and clears them for signing. Reviews project reports, records
project completion, reviews submitted college reports, and prepares RMS.

Research Coordinator:
Handles the college's LREC feedback and College Accomplishment Report.
Can also help prepare and release signing documents for eligible projects.
The Coordinator does not replace the Head's proposal decision.

Research Office Secretary:
Checks quarterly project reports before forwarding them to the Head.
Requests researcher corrections when needed. Can also prepare and release
eligible signed proposal documents and Notices to Proceed.

Project secretary:
A member of the research team who helps prepare the monitoring budget.
This is a different responsibility from Research Office Secretary. Being
assigned as project secretary does not grant office report-review powers.

GAD Office, co-evaluator, committee members, and signatories:
Supply assessments, comments, or signatures. The current app records their
work through the authorized Head/office actions; it does not give every
external assessor a separate review workspace.

An account can have several roles. Its selected workspace determines which
actions are available. College and project access restrictions still apply.


3. PROPOSAL REVIEW, STEP BY STEP

Step 1: The Faculty owner prepares the proposal.
Complete the project details and these five papers:
  - Detailed Research Proposal
  - Work Plan
  - Line-Item Budget
  - Expense Breakdown
  - Curriculum Vitae for the team

ATHENA also generates the GAD Checklist and Initial Screening Form.
Use "Prepare seven PDFs", inspect them, then "Turn in proposal".
Preparing or downloading PDFs does not submit the proposal. Submission
creates a numbered submitted version. Only the draft owner can submit it.
Readiness checks and configured project-capacity limits can block submission.

Step 2: The Research Head checks the submitted version.
The Head reads the papers and can annotate them. The main choices are:
  - Request revisions
  - Reject proposal
  - Clear for GAD assessment

Saving a comment alone does not send a revision request. The Head must
record/send the decision. Opening the proposal as Head also ends the owner's
unrestricted ability to update it before review has started.

Step 3: The Head records the GAD assessment.
The GAD Office supplies the completed assessment. The Head uploads it,
records or reads its score, and confirms the project and verifier signature.
The current scoring outcomes are:
  - Below 4: returned
  - 4 to below 8: conditional pass, requiring revision
  - 8 to below 15: passed
  - 15 to 20: commended

A conditional pass does not unlock the next review. The current version
needs a passing assessment and signature confirmation to proceed.

Step 4: The Head records the co-evaluator's evaluation.
This uses the completed Initial Screening Form, including its Narrative
Evaluation and recommendation: For Endorsement, Minor Revision, or Major
Revision. A passing GAD assessment plus For Endorsement allows the Head to
queue the proposal for LREC. Minor/Major Revision needs correction and a
new endorsed evaluation. Recording an assessment alone does not send the
proposal onward or send it back to the owner; the decision is separate.

Step 5: The proposal goes through LREC presentation and review.
"For email to LREC" places it in "Awaiting LREC presentation". That action
queues the proposal; it does not itself send an email to LREC.

After the presentation, the Coordinator or Head records that it is complete.
The Coordinator can record/send committee comments for their college.
The Head decides whether to request revisions, reject, or clear for signing.

Step 6: Revisions return to the stage that requested them.
The owner reads each requested change, edits the requested papers, and
answers the comments. A change response needs its location in the paper;
leaving a requested paper unchanged requires an explanation.

Submitting the revision creates the next version. Earlier versions remain
available, and unchanged papers can carry forward. A private edited draft
is not yet the submitted revision. GAD-stage revisions require reassessment;
eligible assessments can carry forward for an LREC revision.

Rejection closes the proposal. A revision request gives the owner a chance
to correct it. These outcomes should not be treated as interchangeable.

Step 7: The Head clears the latest version for final signing.
Office staff collect the required signed proposal papers and prepare the
Notice to Proceed. The signing package checks the Detailed Proposal, Work
Plan, Line-Item Budget, GAD Checklist, and Initial Screening Form. Valid GAD
and co-evaluator assessment copies can satisfy their corresponding papers.

The office saves the Notice details, downloads the unsigned notice, obtains
the required signatures, and uploads/releases the signed copy. The required
signed proposal papers must be complete before this release succeeds.

Release records the proposal as Approved, issues the Notice to Proceed,
and opens project monitoring. "Final signing" alone does not open monitoring.
If signing is returned for revision, the old final signed copies are
superseded. The Detailed Proposal, Work Plan, and Line-Item Budget must
be signed again.


4. QUARTERLY PROJECT REPORT REVIEW

For each project reporting period, there are two separate reports:
  - Monitoring Tool: approved Work Plan activities, accomplishment, progress,
    supporting evidence, and budget use.
  - Progress Report: narrative explanation and structured accomplishments.

They describe the same project period but do not replace each other.

Project periods run in three-month blocks from the later of the approved
start date and Notice issue date. The final period can be shorter.
For example, if both dates allow a February 1 start, project Q1 runs from
February 1 through April 30. Always read the displayed date range.

Drafting and previewing can happen early. Official quarterly preparation
and submission open during the final seven days of the period. The last
day is the deadline; overdue submission remains possible afterward.

The team fills the report and supplies the required accomplishment proof.
The owner prepares the official PDF, reviews it, and submits it. If a project
secretary is assigned, the Monitoring Tool's budget must also be prepared.
Authorized team members can help complete that budget.

The review handoff is:
  1. Submitted report waits with the college's Research Office Secretary.
  2. The Secretary forwards the same report to the Head, or requests
     corrections from the researcher with remarks.
  3. The Head accepts the forwarded report, or returns concerns to the
     Research Office Secretary.
  4. When concerns return, the Secretary decides whether to request
     researcher corrections or forward it again.
  5. A researcher correction produces a replacement version for that same
     reporting period and goes through the Secretary again.

Earlier submitted versions and their official PDFs remain in history.

The quarterly table's main labels mean:
  Office review: the Research Office Secretary acts next.
  Head review: the Research Head acts next; it is not accepted yet.
  Corrections requested: the researcher must prepare the requested revision.
  Accepted: the Head accepted that report; the project is not thereby complete.

A Head return does not immediately unlock researcher editing. The Secretary
must first turn the concerns into a researcher correction request.


5. TERMINAL REPORT AND PROJECT COMPLETION

The Terminal Report is the end-of-project report. Its official preparation
and submission open the day after the project ends. Required quarterly
Monitoring Tools and Progress Reports must have been submitted without
outstanding researcher correction requests.

The Terminal Report goes directly to the Research Head, bypassing the
Secretary forwarding stage. The Head can review it or request researcher
corrections directly. After reviewing the latest submitted Terminal Report,
the Head uploads its fully signed PDF.

The Head then explicitly marks the project Completed and confirms that
completion is final. The app checks:
  - The project end has passed.
  - The latest Head-visible Monitoring Tool shows 100% progress.
  - All required quarterly report pairs have been submitted without
    outstanding researcher corrections.
  - No current quarterly report is still at the Secretary stage.
  - The latest Terminal Report has been reviewed.
  - Its fully signed PDF is stored.

100% alone produces "Completion pending"; it does not close the project.
Completed projects keep their records, but monitoring becomes read-only.

Current-rule detail: the completion gate does not separately require every
quarterly report to have been Head-accepted. A quarterly report pending at
the Head stage can satisfy the quarterly submission checks. The Terminal
Report itself must be reviewed and signed. This is an app rule to discuss
if the intended policy is that every quarterly report must be accepted first.


6. COLLEGE REPORTS AND THE CAMPUS RMS REPORT

The Coordinator prepares one College Accomplishment Report per college per
calendar quarter. Calendar Q1 is January-March, Q2 April-June, Q3 July-
September, and Q4 October-December. These can differ from project periods.

Project research rows and eligible faculty proof appear automatically.
The Coordinator enters activity rows such as presentations, publications,
and seminars, and can add supported manual/external research or IP entries.
Presentation, publication, and seminar rows need their own proof image.

Faculty proof is eligible when it comes from a submitted Monitoring/Progress
Report visible to the Head, belongs to that college, and the report's reporting
date falls within the selected calendar quarter. This uses the reporting date,
not the upload or submission date. Private drafts and reports still at the
Secretary stage do not feed this evidence. Forwarded does not mean accepted.

College submission opens after the calendar quarter ends. It freezes a
numbered snapshot and preserves copies of its evidence. Submitted reports
are locked for editing. The Head can return the latest submission with a
reason; the Coordinator corrects it and resubmits a new version. Earlier
submitted versions and proof remain available. There is no separate College
"Approve" action in the current implementation.

For RMS, the Head consolidates the latest submitted, unreturned College
snapshots into three output categories:
  - Paper presentations
  - Publications
  - Intellectual property assets

The Head reviews missing fields, evidence links, campus information and
signatories, saves a draft, and exports the Excel workbook. General research
lists, seminars, and generic proof do not automatically become RMS rows.
RMS record rows come from the corresponding College activity/IP entries.

A returned College report is excluded until resubmitted. If College sources
change while the Head edits RMS, the app blocks saving the stale form until
it is refreshed. Partial drafts and partial Excel exports are allowed, so a
successful download does not mean all fields are complete or approved.
RMS currently has no further submission or approval stage.


7. A SHORT EXAMPLE TO TEST UNDERSTANDING

Imagine a fictional project called Clean Water Research.

Its owner prepares seven PDFs and turns in the proposal. The Head requests
a clearer method. The owner submits Version 2 with an answer to that comment.
The Head clears it for GAD. A passing signed GAD assessment and an endorsed
co-evaluator evaluation allow LREC presentation/review. After committee
comments are resolved, the Head clears signing. The office releases the
required signed papers and signed Notice; monitoring now opens.

At the end of its first project period, the owner submits the Monitoring
Tool and Progress Report. The Secretary forwards them. The Head returns a
budget concern to the Secretary. The researcher cannot revise that report
yet. The Secretary requests correction, the owner submits the revised
version, the Secretary forwards it again, and the Head accepts it.

The Coordinator can use eligible submitted, Head-visible evidence in the
appropriate calendar-quarter College report. A presentation must still have
its own College activity row and proof to become an RMS presentation row.
The Head can return the College report; it then stays out of RMS until the
Coordinator resubmits it.

Finally, the project reaches 100%. It still needs the remaining completion
checks, including a reviewed Terminal Report and its signed PDF. The Head's
explicit completion action moves it into Completed Projects.


8. IS THE SYSTEM EASY TO UNDERSTAND?

The main sequence can be understood, but these distinctions need clear UI
wording. They are clarity recommendations, not workflow changes made here:

  - Always show "Waiting for [role]" and the next action beside a status.
  - Keep Research Coordinator, Research Office Secretary, and project
    secretary clearly distinguished.
  - Explain why both a Monitoring Tool and Progress Report are required.
  - Display report dates beside Q1/Q2 so project and calendar quarters are
    not confused.
  - Explain that a Head concern returns to the Secretary before the
    researcher can revise a quarterly report.
  - Replace "For email to LREC" with wording that does not suggest an email
    was sent automatically.
  - Distinguish initial Head checking from the co-evaluator's form named
    Initial Screening Form.
  - Keep Draft, Prepared PDF, Submitted, Accepted, and Completed separate.
  - Decide whether project completion should require acceptance of every
    quarterly report, rather than the current submission-based checks.

The Research Assistant can explain records and suggest wording. Applying
AI suggestions is separate from submitting work. It does not replace a
reviewer's decision or grant approval. The current app also does not expose
a separate similarity-check review stage.

Folders help people find records; changing icon size, switching Details
view, or arranging personal folders does not change a review decision.
